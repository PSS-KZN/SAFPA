## 1. Overview & Executive Summary
SAFPA is entering a period where leadership, member value, policy engagement, and industry
relevance matter more than ever. Publicly, SAFPA is positioning itself around collaboration, policy
dialogue, growth, and strategic partnerships, and current leadership has spoken about building a
SAFPA that adds value to members’ businesses. This proposal is designed to turn that leadership
mandate into a practical, scalable programme with measurable business impact for members.
We propose that SAFPA establish a national member digitisation and business enablement
programme that uses the association’s aggregate buying power to help funeral parlours modernise
in a way that is affordable, practical, and controlled by the industry itself. The programme would
provide member businesses with access to websites, mobile apps, connectivity, premium collection,
policy administration, and funeral service management tools, supported by a SAFPA-led commercial
and governance framework.
This is not simply a software rollout. It is a sector-strengthening initiative. It is intended to help
SAFPA members collect premiums more efficiently, reduce revenue leakage, manage policies and
service delivery more professionally, improve customer trust, and prepare members for a more
formal and more demanding operating environment.
The proposed model recognises that SAFPA members are not all the same. Some are small
community-based schemes with fewer than 100 members. Others are large, sophisticated operators
with tens or hundreds of thousands of lives on book. Some are digitally mature; many are not. Some
operate in metropolitan environments; others work in rural and region-specific contexts with unique
cultural and operational nuances. The programme is therefore designed as a modular, tiered
platform rather than a one-size-fits-all solution.
## 1.1 Why This Matters Now
The strategic case for SAFPA to lead this initiative is strong.
First, the regulatory and policy environment is moving toward greater professionalism, transparency,
and consumer protection. The South African Law Reform Commission’s review of laws regulating
funeral parlours and related services notes that the sector is governed by multiple pieces of
legislation across different departments and municipalities, proposes an ombud scheme and a code
of conduct, and highlights gaps such as the lack of compulsory qualifications or statutory restrictions
on who may operate as a funeral director. The draft code also emphasises transparent pricing,
itemised quotations, complaints processes, protection of confidential information, and
comprehensive record-keeping.
Second, the insurance and collections side of the market is under increasing scrutiny. The Insurance
Act introduced a dedicated class of life insurance business for funeral policies together with a

proportionate framework for microinsurers. In its 2024/25 Regulatory Actions Report, the FSCA
states that more than 70% of new cases involved unregistered financial services and insurance
businesses, and it identifies unregistered insurance in the funeral parlour industry as a specific focus
area, including self-underwriting and unauthorised premium collection from clients.
Third, SAFPA itself is publicly leaning into a bigger leadership role. Its 2026 convention is positioned
as a platform for collaboration, policy dialogue, networking, and growth, bringing together funeral
practitioners, suppliers, regulators, government leaders, and partners. This proposal gives SAFPA a
concrete industry-building programme that matches that public ambition.
In short, the sector is being pulled toward higher standards. The question is whether that transition
will be shaped for the benefit of funeral parlours, or imposed on them from outside. This proposal
argues that SAFPA should lead that transition on behalf of its members.

1.2 The Strategic Opportunity for SAFPA
SAFPA has a unique advantage: collective scale.
Individually, many funeral parlours cannot negotiate affordable technology, payment solutions,
connectivity, compliance support, or systems integration. Collectively, SAFPA can.
That means SAFPA can create a programme that does four things at once:
- It lowers the cost of digitisation for members.
- It improves the operating quality of member businesses.
- It strengthens SAFPA’s relevance and value proposition.
- It gives the sector a credible, industry-owned path to formalisation and growth.
This is the central strategic proposition:
SAFPA should use its aggregate buying power to build a shared digital and commercial platform
that helps member funeral parlours grow, professionalise, and compete more effectively —
without giving up control of their businesses or customer relationships.



## 1.3 Product Name
SAFPA Funeral Parlour Operating System (FPOS)
## 1.4. Short Product Statement
SAFPA FPOS is a multi-tenant SaaS platform that enables funeral parlours to manage members,
policies, premium collections, funeral service workflows, branches, digital presence, and reporting,
while giving SAFPA structured oversight and a scalable digitisation programme for its membership.
## 1.5 Product Summary
SAFPA FPOS is a multi-tenant digital platform that enables SAFPA to digitise its member funeral
parlours by providing a shared, scalable system for:
- member and policy administration
- premium collection and reconciliation
- funeral service and case management
- branded websites and digital presence
- reporting, support, and association-level oversight
The platform must support a wide range of funeral parlour operators, from small community
schemes with roughly 50 members to larger operators managing up to 300,000+ lives, while
remaining affordable, modular, and usable in low-bandwidth environments.
1.6 Purpose of This PRD
This document defines the product vision, goals, users, scope, requirements, MVP, non-functional
requirements, and success criteria for the SAFPA platform. It is intended to align business, product,
design, and engineering stakeholders before development begins.

- Background and Problem Statement
SAFPA members operate in a fragmented market with widely differing levels of digital maturity,
operational sophistication, and scale. Common industry problems include:
- paper-based or spreadsheet-based member and policy administration
- unreliable premium collection and weak reconciliation
- revenue leakage from missed or failed payments
- low visibility into arrears, lapse risk, and active member books
- limited digital presence and inconsistent customer engagement
- weak service workflow management during funeral operations
- poor reporting and limited readiness for formalisation or compliance


Many funeral parlours cannot afford suitable technology on their own. SAFPA seeks to use its
aggregate buying power to provide members with a shared digital platform and commercially
negotiated services that strengthen member businesses without taking ownership of their customers
or data.

## 3. Product Vision
Build a SAFPA-led digital operating system for funeral parlours that improves collections, control, and
service delivery.
The platform should become the core day-to-day business system for participating members,
enabling them to manage policies, collect premiums, run funeral operations, and serve customers
through digital channels.

## 4. Product Goals
## 4.1 Primary Goals
The product must help participating funeral parlours:
- collect premiums more efficiently and reduce revenue leakage
- manage members, dependants, and policies accurately
- manage funeral service workflows from notification to closure
- improve customer visibility and trust through digital presence
- support branches, field teams, and regional operating differences
- provide SAFPA with structured, aggregate insight into adoption and performance
## 4.2 Business Goals
The platform should also help SAFPA:
- improve its member value proposition
- create a sustainable commercial model for digitisation
- enable a tiered adoption path for different member sizes
- support partnerships with banks, payment providers, telcos, and other ecosystem partners







## 4.3 Initial Success Metrics
These are proposed launch targets and should be refined during discovery:
- 80% of pilot parlours actively using the platform weekly within 90 days of go-live
- 95% of successful premium transactions automatically reconciled on the same day
- 50% reduction in manual premium follow-up work for pilot members
- 70% of pilot funeral cases managed digitally end-to-end
- website go-live for a new parlour within 3 business days of configuration approval
- member/policy import completed within 2 business days for standard onboarding cases

- Non-Goals
The first release is not intended to be:
- a full general ledger or accounting ERP
- a full insurance carrier core system
- a payroll or HR system
- a vehicle telematics platform
- a sophisticated actuarial engine
- a consumer marketplace for funeral comparisons
These may become future integrations or later modules, but are not part of initial scope.

## 9. Product Principles
The platform must be:
- multi-tenant
- role-based and secure
- configurable by parlour and branch
- mobile-first where practical
- API-first for integrations
- usable in low-connectivity environments
- white-label capable for member branding
- modular so smaller parlours can start simple
- association-led but member-controlled

## 10. Key User Journeys
10.1 Onboard a New SAFPA Member Parlour
- SAFPA approves participation
- system creates parlour tenant
- branches and users are configured
- branding and website template are selected
- products and policy rules are configured
- data import is completed
- payment provider is connected
- parlour goes live

10.2 Register a New Policyholder
- lead is captured online, in branch, or by agent
- member record is created
- dependants and beneficiaries are added
- package is selected
- payment method is chosen
- required documents are uploaded
- policy becomes active based on business rules

- welcome communication is sent
## 10.3 Collect Monthly Premiums
- premium due date is reached
- collection batch is generated
- payment provider attempts collection
- results are returned to platform
- successful transactions are applied
- failed transactions are flagged
- arrears and reminders are updated
- reports reflect latest status
10.4 Manage a Funeral Case
- death notice is logged
- policyholder is identified or new service case is opened
- coordinator is assigned
- documents and eligibility checks are completed
- service tasks and dates are scheduled
- staff, suppliers, and resources are assigned
- family communications are logged
- service is delivered
- case is closed and archived





## 11. Functional Requirements
11.1 SAFPA Administration
FR-001 The system must allow SAFPA to create and manage participating funeral parlours.
FR-002 The system must allow SAFPA to assign region/province to each parlour.
FR-003 The system must allow SAFPA to assign subscription packages or service tiers.
FR-004 The system must provide aggregate dashboards across participating parlours.
FR-005 The system must track adoption status, onboarding progress, and active usage by parlour.
FR-006 SAFPA users must not have unrestricted operational access to all tenant data unless explicitly
authorised by policy and permissions.
FR-007 The system must support publication of notices, training resources, and partner services for
participating members.
## 11.2 Parlour Administration
FR-010 Each parlour must have its own tenant space with isolated data.
FR-011 Each parlour must be able to create and manage multiple branches.
FR-012 Each parlour must be able to configure branding, logo, colours, contact details, and business
profile.
FR-013 Each parlour must be able to create and manage users and assign roles.
FR-014 Each parlour must be able to configure products, packages, and pricing.
FR-015 Each parlour must be able to configure templates for customer communications.
11.3 Websites and Digital Presence
FR-020 The platform must generate a branded website for each participating parlour from
configurable templates.
FR-021 The website must support custom domains or SAFPA-hosted subdomains.
FR-022 The website must allow publication of service packages, branch details, and contact
information.
FR-023 The website must include lead capture and enquiry forms.
FR-024 Website forms must push captured leads into the platform.
FR-025 The website must be mobile responsive and manageable by non-technical users.
11.4 CRM and Lead Management
FR-030 The system must capture leads from website forms, branches, and field agents.
FR-031 The system must assign lead owners and track lead stage.
FR-032 The system must allow conversion of a lead into a customer/member record.
FR-033 The system must capture the source of each lead for reporting.
11.5 Policy and Member Management
FR-040 The system must support creation and management of customer/member profiles.
FR-041 The system must support dependants, beneficiaries, and family relationships.
FR-042 The system must support creation of policies/plans linked to a member.
FR-043 The system must support configurable policy/package rules, including waiting periods and
status transitions.
FR-044 The system must support the following policy states at minimum: draft, pending, active,
suspended, lapsed, reinstated, cancelled, closed.


FR-045 The system must track premium amount, billing frequency, next due date, arrears, and
payment status.
FR-046 The system must support documents linked to a member or policy.
FR-047 The system must support bulk import of members and policies from CSV/Excel.
FR-048 The system should support group or burial society style enrolment structures in a later MVP
enhancement or phase 2.
11.6 Premium Collection and Reconciliation
FR-050 The system must integrate with at least one payment provider in MVP.
FR-051 The system must support multiple payment methods, including debit order, EFT, card, and
manual branch capture.
FR-052 The system must generate premium billing events based on configured schedules.
FR-053 The system must record successful, failed, reversed, and pending payment transactions.
FR-054 The system must automatically apply successful payments to the correct policy/account.
FR-055 The system must generate receipts for successful payments.
FR-056 The system must surface failed transactions for follow-up and retry.
FR-057 The system must calculate and display arrears and overdue balances.
FR-058 The system must support import or matching of provider settlement/reconciliation files
where direct callbacks are insufficient.
FR-059 The system must maintain a full audit trail for payment events.
11.7 Funeral Service and Case Management
FR-060 The system must allow users to log a death notice or new funeral service request.
FR-061 The system must support both policy-linked service cases and cash/private service cases.
FR-062 The system must create a funeral case record with status tracking.
FR-063 The system must allow assignment of a case owner/coordinator.
FR-064 The system must support task checklists for service delivery.
FR-065 The system must support scheduling of funeral-related events and milestones.
FR-066 The system must support assignment of branch staff, vehicles, or suppliers where enabled.
FR-067 The system must allow documents and notes to be attached to a funeral case.
FR-068 The system must maintain a communication log per case.
FR-069 The system must allow closure and archiving of completed cases.
## 11.8 Communications

FR-071 The system must send policy status communications.
FR-072 The system must send funeral case updates where enabled.
FR-073 The system must support templated messaging by parlour.

FR-075 WhatsApp support should be added in phase 2 or earlier if commercially critical.

- Non-Functional Requirements
## 12.1 Security
- role-based access control is required
- tenant data isolation is required
- encryption in transit and at rest is required
- audit logging is required for sensitive actions
- admin accounts should support MFA
- secure password handling and session management are required
12.2 Privacy and Compliance
- personal data handling must support POPIA-aligned practices
- the platform must store consent and communication preference where applicable
- sensitive documents must be access-controlled
- financial and policy changes must be traceable
## 12.3 Performance
- standard user actions should generally complete within 2 seconds under expected load
- dashboard loads should generally complete within 5 seconds for normal data volumes
- import and reconciliation jobs may be asynchronous but must provide progress feedback
## 12.4 Availability
- system should target high availability during business hours
- automated backups are required
- disaster recovery procedures are required
## 12.5 Scalability
The solution must support:
- many funeral parlour tenants
- many branches per tenant
- millions of records over time
- tenants as small as 50 members and as large as 300,000+ members
12.6 Connectivity and Device Constraints
- primary admin experience should work well on low-cost laptops and Android devices
- public and staff workflows must be bandwidth-aware
- offline capture for selected field workflows is phase 2 unless essential to pilot success

- MVP Definition
The MVP is the smallest release that allows SAFPA to onboard participating parlours and lets a
parlour manage core commercial and operational workflows digitally.
MVP Must Include
- SAFPA admin console
- parlour and branch setup
- user and role management
- templated parlour websites
- lead capture
- member and policy management
- payment scheduling and transaction recording
- receipts and arrears tracking
- funeral case creation and status tracking
- document upload
- basic notifications by SMS/email
- basic operational and management reporting
- CSV/Excel import tools
MVP Excludes
- advanced customer mobile app
- deep offline mode
- full supplier marketplace
- advanced automation rules
- enterprise-grade benchmarking
- insurer-grade claims processing

## 18. Assumptions
This PRD assumes:
- SAFPA will act as programme owner or sponsor
- member parlours will adopt the product in tiers
- not all parlours will require all modules on day one
- at least one payments partner can be secured for pilot
- parlour data migration will often come from Excel or manual records
- initial rollout will prioritise practicality over full sophistication





- Risks and Mitigations
Risk 1: Low adoption by smaller parlours
Mitigation: keep MVP simple, mobile-friendly, and affordable; provide onboarding support and
templates.
Risk 2: Data quality issues during migration
Mitigation: provide import templates, validations, duplicate checks, and assisted onboarding.
Risk 3: Distrust about data ownership
Mitigation: make tenant ownership and access rules explicit in contracts and product permissions.
Risk 4: Payment integration complexity
Mitigation: start with one strong provider in pilot and add abstraction for future providers.
Risk 5: Overbuilding too early
Mitigation: phase delivery and keep MVP tightly scoped around collections, policy admin, and
service workflow.

- MVP Acceptance Criteria
The MVP will be considered ready for pilot when the following are true:
- SAFPA can create and manage participating parlours in the platform.
- A parlour can configure its branches, users, branding, and products.
- A parlour can import customers and policies from CSV/Excel.
- Staff can create and update member and policy records.
- Premium transactions can be recorded and reconciled.
- Receipts and arrears status are visible in the system.
- A parlour website can be published from a template.
- Staff can capture and track funeral cases from opening to closure.
- SMS/email communications can be sent and logged.
- Role-based access and tenant data isolation work as designed.

- Open Questions for Discovery
The following must be confirmed before detailed design and build:
- Will the product support only administration and operations, or also regulated insurance-
linked workflows?
- What are the exact policy lifecycle rules, including grace periods and reinstatements?
- Which payment providers are preferred for pilot and national rollout?
- Does every participating parlour receive a website, or only specific subscription tiers?
- Must customer self-service be included in MVP for pilot success?
- How much case costing and profitability tracking is required?
- Should group schemes and burial societies be first-class entities in MVP?
- What reporting detail is SAFPA permitted to see by policy and contract?
- What service levels are expected from the delivery partner?
- Should the mobile app be shared SAFPA-branded, white-label per parlour, or both?

## 24. Closing Statement
This PRD establishes the initial product direction for the SAFPA Funeral Parlour Operating System and
is intended to guide design, scoping, technical planning, and phased delivery. The platform is
envisioned as a practical, scalable, and commercially sustainable solution that enables SAFPA
member funeral parlours to improve collections, strengthen policy and service administration, and
modernise their operations without losing control of their businesses or customer relationships.
As a working document, this PRD should be refined through stakeholder engagement, technical
discovery, pilot feedback, and implementation planning. The objective is not simply to build
software, but to deliver a trusted digital foundation that supports the growth, resilience, and
professionalism of funeral parlours across South Africa under SAFPA’s leadership.
